Pianoverse

Privacy policy

Updated 15 October 2026 (crash reports added)

Pianoverse is an app for keeping track of the pianos you sell and rent out. It is made by Gurleen Singh. This page says what the app keeps about you and the people you rent to, why, where it is stored, and how to have it deleted.

Questions about it: pianoverse.app.dev@gmail.com.

What Pianoverse keeps

Your account

  • Your name and email address.
  • Your password, if you sign up with one. Appwrite, the service that runs sign-in, keeps it in a form that can’t be read back, so nobody can see it.
  • Your profile picture, if you add one, or your Google photo if you sign in with Google.

If you choose Sign in with Google, Google gives Pianoverse your name, email address and profile photo, and nothing else.

What you enter

  • Your pianos: their names, makes, models and other details, prices, dates and notes.
  • Photos of your pianos, taken with the camera or chosen on your phone. Only the photos you pick are uploaded.
  • Rentals and sales: the customer’s or buyer’s name and phone number, the dates and the prices.
  • Rent payments: the amount, the day, who paid and your note.
  • Tuning and service records for your pianos: the day, what was done, the cost and your note, and how often each piano is tuned.

Some of this is about other people: your customers. Enter only details they are happy for you to keep, for running your rentals. Pianoverse uses them for nothing but showing them to you.

Which paper forms you use

When you open a paper form, or save, print or share its PDF, the app notes which form it was (for example the rent card), what you did, how many pages the PDF had, when, the app’s version and whether your phone is Android or an iPhone, with your account. This shows which forms are used, so the useful ones get better. It never includes what is on the forms: they are blank, and your letterhead and business details stay on your phone. A count made without a connection waits on your phone and is sent later.

When the app breaks

If the app crashes or hits an error, it sends a crash report so the problem can be fixed: what went wrong and where in the app’s code, the screen you were on, your phone’s model and system version, the app’s version, and your account’s ID (not your name or email). Phone numbers and email addresses are masked before a report leaves your phone, and it never includes a screenshot, what was on the screen or what you typed. Reports are only sent by the app installed on a phone, and go to Sentry (below).

Only on your phone

These never leave your phone:

  • A copy of your piano list, so it shows without a connection.
  • Your theme and app lock settings.
  • Your business details and letterhead for the paper forms, with its logo or image, and the PDFs you make (until you share or save them somewhere else).
  • Your rental and tuning reminders, which your phone schedules itself.
  • The files you download from Account, Your data.

What Pianoverse doesn’t collect

Your location, contacts, calls, microphone or advertising ID. There are no ads, no advertising services, and no tracking across other apps or websites. The only use of the app that is counted is which paper forms you use, and the only reports are crash reports, both described above.

How it is used

Only to run the app for you: to show your pianos, rentals and payments, to remind you when a rental ends, and to make the files you download. The paper form counts are used only to decide which forms to improve. None of it is sold, rented out or shared for advertising, and it isn’t used for anything else.

Where it is stored, and who else handles it

  • Appwrite (appwrite.io) stores your account, pianos, payments, photos and paper form counts, and runs signing in.
  • Google signs you in, if you choose Sign in with Google.
  • Sentry (sentry.io) receives the crash reports, keeps them in the United States, and deletes them after its retention period (at most 90 days).
  • Expo (expo.dev) delivers updates to the app. When the app checks for one, Expo receives the app’s version and basic details of your phone, not your account or what you entered.

They handle the data only to provide these services. Everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS).

When you call or message a customer from the app, it opens your phone’s dialer or WhatsApp with the message written for you. You send it yourself; Pianoverse sends nothing.

What the app asks your phone for

  • Camera: to take photos of your pianos, and of yourself for your profile.
  • Photos: to choose photos on your phone. Only the ones you pick reach the app.
  • Notifications: for rental reminders.
  • Fingerprint, face or screen lock: only if you turn on App lock. Your phone checks it’s you, and the app only learns whether it worked.

Who sees it

The app shows your pianos, customers and payments only to you, signed in to your account. As the one who runs Pianoverse, I look at the paper form counts to see which forms are used; I can reach the rest of the stored data only to keep the service working or when you ask me for help.

How long it is kept, and deleting it

Everything is kept until you delete it or your account. You can delete a piano, with its photos and payments, at any time. Signing out clears your letterhead, business details, PDFs and any paper form counts not yet sent from that phone.

To delete your account and everything in it, the paper form counts included, open the app and go to Account, Delete account. It happens straight away. Without the app, you can ask by email. Deleted data can’t be recovered. Copies in Appwrite’s own backups, if there are any, expire on their own.

Your rights

You can see your data in the app, download it (Account, Your data), change it and delete it. For anything else, such as a copy of everything stored about you, write to pianoverse.app.dev@gmail.com. You will get an answer within 30 days.

Children

Pianoverse is for running a piano business. It isn’t meant for children under 13, and doesn’t knowingly keep their data.
